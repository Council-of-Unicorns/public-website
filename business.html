The plan

Substrate builds the RPU — the Robot Processing Unit, an accelerator specialized to run world models at 40 watts on the edge.

  • Our first product targets a realistic >5× improvement in power efficiency, utilizing proven methods to target the lowest-hanging fruit. Reference the technical roadmap.
  • Long-term vision: co-design our hardware with the converged model architectures of the future, achieving a >15× improvement in power efficiency. We will become the computational substrate for physical intelligence.

Philosophy

At SpaceX, we learned that there is no such thing as pure software. Fundamentally, software is constrained by hardware. GPUs are impressive, but even at full utilization they cannot run state-of-the-art world-action models on the edge. We must have the imagination to optimize the system as a whole, and create a specialized NPU for this workload. This will unlock the ChatGPT moment for robotics.

We also don’t want to live in a world where our robots are puppets controlled by datacenters hundreds or thousands of miles away, at the mercy of network transients. Besides the reliability issues, remote inference cannot scalably achieve human-like reaction latencies. And the cost of renting expensive datacenter racks can quickly surpass the BOM cost of the robot itself.

To keep ourselves accountable, we follow Elon’s algorithm.

  1. Make the requirements less dumb. Challenge every assumption. The industry assumes these workloads cannot run on the edge, and therefore require complex remote-inference stacks.
  2. Delete the part. Most of a GPU’s area and energy exists so it can run anything.
  3. Simplify and optimize — only what survives deletion: one dataflow, memory managed by the compiler, and optimization spent where the joules actually go.
  4. Accelerate cycle time. Invest in simulation and fail fast. Aggressively pursue tapeout timelines.
  5. Automate. Our long-term mission is to create a company-as-a-machine that allows us to capitalize on the convergence of model architecture and co-design an unbeatable chip.

The Long Term Vision

Once the first chip ships, we turn the whole company toward co-design: building each new generation of silicon around the model architecture the industry is converging on. Three curves are meeting at once — architectures are converging, model compression is maturing, and memory is moving onto the compute die.

A chip built for a known architecture, not a moving target, reaches far past the first product. Our instrument bounds it above 15× a Jetson-class part — a range the simulation page publishes honestly, as a bound rather than a promise. That is enough to run the largest world-action models of the future in real time and onboard, far beyond what a general-purpose chip can do. It is how we become the computational substrate for physical intelligence.

The two questions we keep asking

Does more onboard compute still buy more capable robots? If frontier models shrink until Thor has headroom to spare, this opportunity weakens substantially. Our current bet is that demand for onboard compute keeps increasing: scaling laws emerging in robot pretraining, growing context windows, and test-time reasoning arriving in robotics. But we re-test that assumption at every stage.

Can the RPU really deliver 3–4×? Our instrument says 2.9–4.2× today, with a floor of 1.9× if everything breaks against us. If physical measurement drags the real answer down toward 1.3×, the cost of integrating a new chip outweighs the benefit, and the one-year decision says stop. That is exactly what it is for.
