RPU — Robot Processing Unit

A Specialized Chip
for Robotics.

The ChatGPT moment of robotics requires true, embodied intelligence that runs onboard with strict, deterministic latency. We are building the RPU (robot processing unit), a processor and compiler optimized for diffusion transformer inference. Our goal is to run more capable robot policies onboard, within a 100‑watt power budget.

Detail

Robot intelligence is growing faster than the compute robots can carry. Larger models offer more capable behavior, but many require datacenter GPUs.

We are building the RPU to bring more of that intelligence onboard. We design around the computation shared by diffusion transformers, with loadable models and schedules that can change as robotics evolves.

01 — The requirement

Real World Deployment
Is Safety Critical

Robots work beside people, handle tools, and carry heavy objects. A delayed reaction can cause harm. Their ability to respond must not depend on a network connection. Remote inference can work for demos, but cannot scale to real world deployment.

Detail

We focus on the fast perception-and-action loop: System 1. It must process new observations and respond with low, predictable latency. Slower planning — System 2 — can use remote compute.

Our reaction-time goal is roughly 200 milliseconds, from observation to action. Inference must fit within that deadline alongside sensing, communication within the robot, and control.

An action generated remotely reflects an earlier observation. Network jitter makes the age of that observation unpredictable. Buffering can smooth motion, but it cannot tell the robot what changed while it waited.

Local safety controllers remain essential. The robot needs an answer from its latest observations and cannot wait to assemble a batch, so we design for batch-one inference under a strict power limit.

Design goals

  • 100 Wonboard power budget
  • ~200 msend-to-end reaction-time goal
  • Batch oneinference from the latest observations
  • ~3×first-silicon performance-per-watt target over Thor
  • ~5×target as the compiler matures
  • Loadablemodels adapt as architectures evolve

Performance figures are targets to validate through simulation and prototyping.

02 — The RPU

Models Have Converged Enough
For Specialization

Robot policies have converged on diffusion transformers. RPU specializes in the repeated matrix and attention operations at their core. A robot has limited power and cooling, so running a more capable model means spending less energy on each inference.

Detail

Diffusion transformers process many tokens together. They reuse weights across those tokens and perform substantial arithmetic within each layer. Even at batch one, these workloads can be limited by compute and power.

The compiler plans computation and data movement in advance. The hardware follows that schedule, keeps partial results close to the arithmetic, and reuses data before fetching it again.

Our design combines a systolic array, low-precision arithmetic, compiler-managed memory, and fused attention. Together, these reduce the energy spent moving data and coordinating execution.

RPU is a companion accelerator. The robot’s existing computer continues to handle sensors, networking, independent safety functions, and conventional control. RPU accelerates policy inference.

Our measure of success is lower energy per completed inference at the required latency and model quality. We target roughly 3× Thor’s performance per watt with first silicon and 5× as the compiler improves.

Weights and execution schedules remain loadable, so developers can update supported models without replacing the chip.

03 — The horizon

Better models and better
hardware, designed together.

Our first chip preserves the flexibility to follow changing models. As architectures stabilize, we can specialize further.

Detail

We will improve how the compiler schedules work, how the memory system supplies data, and how the hardware executes common operations.

Over time, closer integration of memory and compute — and dedicated circuitry for stable parts of the model — could unlock larger gains. Our long-term target is roughly 15× better energy efficiency through hardware–model codesign.

The goal stays the same: fit more intelligence onboard so robots can respond reliably in the physical world.
